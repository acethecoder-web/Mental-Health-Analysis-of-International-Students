# Mental-Health-Analysis-of-International-Students
This SQL data analysis project explores factors associated with depression among international students. Using exploratory analysis and aggregation techniques, the study examines patterns across region, gender, age, educational level, and suicide ideation to identify key mental health trends.

## Business Question

- Which factors are associated with higher depression scores?

## Questions

- Which region has the highest average depression score?
- Does gender affect depression levels?
- Are undergraduates more depressed than graduates?
- Does suicide ideation correspond to higher depression scores?
- Which age group has the highest average depression?

# Key Findings

Based on exploratory data analysis conducted on the dataset, the following insights were identified:

## 1. Regional Analysis

Among the regions represented in the dataset, Southeast Asia (SEA) recorded the highest average depression score compared with:

Southeast Asia (SEA)
Japan (JAP)
East Asia (EA)
South Asia (SA)

This suggests that students from the SEA region within the dataset exhibited relatively higher levels of depression.

## 2. Gender and Depression

The analysis indicates that female students recorded a higher number of depression cases than male students in the dataset.

While this finding highlights a difference between genders, further analysis would be required to determine the underlying factors contributing to this pattern.

## 3. Education Level and Depression

The data shows that undergraduate students had a higher number of depression cases compared to graduate students.

This may suggest that undergraduate students experience challenges that are associated with higher depression levels, although additional investigation is needed to draw broader conclusions.

## 4. Suicide Ideation and Depression

The results did not show a direct relationship between suicide ideation and a higher number of depression cases.

Within this dataset, students who reported no suicide ideation represented a larger portion of the students classified as depressed. However, this observation may be influenced by the distribution of respondents in the dataset and should be interpreted with caution.

## 5. Age Group Analysis

Students between the ages of 18 and 23 accounted for the highest number of depression cases observed in the dataset.

This indicates that younger students may represent a more vulnerable group within the population studied.

# Conclusion

The analysis identified several factors associated with depression among international students, including region, gender, educational level, and age group. The findings suggest that students from Southeast Asia, female students, undergraduates, and individuals aged 18 to 23 exhibited relatively higher levels of depression within the dataset. These insights can serve as a starting point for deeper investigation into the factors influencing student mental health and well-being.
